### Social Security Administration

#### Supplemental Security Income (SSI) \*

- **Eligibility:** SSI is a needs-based program for people with limited income and resources who are either age 65 or older, blind, or disabled (no work history required).
- **Benefit Amount:** SSI provides a fixed federal maximum payment (approximately $967 per month for individuals in 2025), which is reduced by countable income.
- **Health Insurance Coverage:** SSI recipients often qualify for automatic Medicaid eligibility in most states.

#### Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)

- **Eligibility:** SSDI requires that you have earned sufficient Social Security work credits through prior employment.
- **Benefit Amount:** SSDI benefits are calculated based on your lifetime earnings, with higher earners receiving larger payments.
- **Health Insurance Coverage:** SSDI recipients become eligible for Medicare after a 24-month waiting period.

#### Social Security Retirement Benefits

- **Eligibility:** Requires at least 40 Social Security work credits earned through prior employment (no recent work requirement, unlike SSDI); available starting at age 62.
- **Benefit Amount:** Calculated based on your lifetime covered earnings, with higher earners receiving larger payments; amount is reduced if claimed before full retirement age (67 for most people).
- **Health Insurance Coverage:** Qualifies you for premium-free Medicare Part A at age 65 if you have the required 40 credits.

### Health Coverage

#### Medicaid \*

- **Eligibility:** Available to low-income individuals with a disability determination (often automatic with SSI approval).
- **Benefits:** Covers medical expenses, doctor visits, hospital care, and often long-term services like in-home care or nursing facilities.
- **Application:** Apply through your state's Medicaid agency; disability rules align with SSA standards.

#### Medicare

- **Eligibility:** Primarily for people age 65 and older, but also available to those under 65 after receiving SSDI disability benefits for 24 months (early-onset dementia can qualify for faster SSDI approval through Compassionate Allowances).
- **Benefits:** Covers hospital stays (Part A), doctor visits/outpatient care (Part B), and prescription drugs (Part D); includes some dementia-related diagnostic tests, care planning, and treatments.
- **Application:** Automatic enrollment after the 24-month SSDI waiting period (or around age 65 if receiving Social Security benefits); contact SSA or medicare.gov for details.

### Nutrition Assistance

#### Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program / Food Stamps (SNAP) \*

- **Eligibility:** Based on low income with special rules for disabled individuals (higher resource limits and medical expense deductions).
- **Benefits:** Provides monthly funds on an EBT card to purchase groceries and food items.
- **Application:** Apply through your state's SNAP office or online portal.

## State Benefits (Georgia)

### Georgia Memory Net (GMN)

- **Eligibility:** Open to all Georgians experiencing memory concerns or diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease or related dementias (including early-onset).
- **Benefits:** Provides early and accurate diagnosis, customized care plans, ongoing management, caregiver support, and referrals to community resources.
- **Access:** Available through Memory Assessment Clinics statewide; contact via gamemorynet.org or your local provider.

### Dementia Care Specialist Program

- **Eligibility:** For individuals living with dementia and their caregivers in Georgia.
- **Benefits:** Offers specialized support, education, referrals, and help building dementia-capable communities through Area Agencies on Aging.
- **Access:** Contact your local Area Agency on Aging (find via aging.georgia.gov or 1-866-552-4464).

### Elderly and Disabled Waiver Program (EDWP) (Via: CCSP & SOURCE)

- **Eligibility:** Medicaid-eligible individuals requiring nursing facility level of care (dementia qualifies); limited slots with some reserved for Alzheimer's/dementia cases.
- **Benefits:** Covers in-home services like personal care, adult day care, respite, home-delivered meals, and alternative living services to avoid nursing homes.
- **Application:** Contact your local Area Agency on Aging (866-552-4464) for screening.

## Local Organizations (Georgia)

### Area Agencies on Aging (AAA) Dementia Services

- **Eligibility:** Georgians with dementia or caregivers seeking local support.
- **Benefits:** Provides referrals to dementia-specific services, support groups, and community resources.
- **Access:** 12 regional AAAs cover the state; locator at aging.georgia.gov.

### Alzheimer's Association Georgia Chapter

- **Eligibility:** Anyone affected by Alzheimer's or dementia in Georgia.
- **Benefits:** Offers free support groups (including early-stage), education programs, 24/7 helpline, and local resources.
- **Access:** Visit [alz.org/georgia](https://alz.org/georgia) or call 800-272-3900.
